Spot what is unusual

The anomaly agent scores signals from Dynatrace, AppDynamics and Splunk with your predictive model, then uses ServiceNow to decide: raise an event, add evidence to an open incident, or hold off during a change window.

Follow a transaction

Give the transaction agent a transaction or correlation id: it pulls the spans and logs from Splunk, AppDynamics, Dynatrace, Jaeger and Tempo, rebuilds the call chain, finds where it failed and whether the cause is upstream or downstream of where it was reported.

Run reliability by SLOs

The reliability agent tracks every SLO's error budget and burn rate, pages on fast burn, says which incidents and changes spent the budget, and sets the release policy: normal, caution, restrict or freeze.

Guard changes before they ship

The change guard scores each upcoming change on blast radius, error budget, change failure rate, current health, collisions and freeze windows, recommends GO, CANARY or HOLD with guardrails, and verifies changes after deploy.

Track ID: the failure point is the deepest failing hop. The origin is its caller when it rejected a bad request (4xx/validation), the dependency it timed out on when that dependency answered slower than the caller's timeout, otherwise the failure point itself. Downstream = the origin is something the reported system calls; upstream = the origin calls it.

Service path: a hop is down with an open P1/P2 or 2+ open incidents, degraded with an open incident or a change in the last 24h. The likely origin is the most broken hop whose own dependencies are less broken.

Error budget = 1 − objective (99.9% → 0.1% of events may fail in the window). Burn rate = how fast that budget is being spent (1× = exactly on budget). Alerts use multi-window burn rates: page when 1h and 5m ≥ 14.4× or 6h and 30m ≥ 6×; ticket when 24h and 2h ≥ 3× or 72h and 6h ≥ 1×. Policy: >50% left normal, 25–50% caution, 0–25% restrict, ≤0 freeze (reliability fixes only). Incident cost = bad events during the incident ÷ the window's budget.
